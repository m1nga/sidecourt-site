# Evidence, learning, and revision

## What counts as progress

More stored text, more retrievals, and more confident summaries are not the learning metric.
Look for a supported change in work: fewer repeat mistakes, a better measured outcome,
less rework without quality loss, or a better decision under the recorded conditions.
State the comparison and its limits; do not manufacture a metric merely to pass a gate.

The loop separates four claims:

- **Record exists:** a statement has provenance, revision and scope.
- **Guidance was used:** a specific revision informed an identified action.
- **Result was observed:** baseline, result and evidence were attached to that use.
- **Lesson remains worth using:** a reviewer judged benefit, counterexamples and scope.

Only the latter claims support learning. An unfinished trial remains pending. Neutral and
inconclusive outcomes are useful records, not failures to hide or evidence for promotion.

## Keep record types distinct

| Type | Meaning | Common mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| fact | Observed condition at a stated source/time | Treating an old snapshot as today's reality |
| decision | Explicit choice in this project | Promoting an assistant suggestion to user approval |
| open | Work/question not resolved | Leaving completed work open indefinitely |
| idea | An option worth revisiting | Calling tentative exploration a requirement |
| preference | Explicit working instruction with scope | Inferring a permanent personality label |
| lesson | A proposed reusable mechanism | Copying a product-specific choice as universal advice |

When repository documents already hold the decision or status, store a short indexed pointer
and verified excerpt. A file's changed hash invalidates automatic freshness, even when the
change may be irrelevant: inspect, then recapture deliberately. A text-only conversation
excerpt cannot detect later contradictions elsewhere; reconcile it against current evidence.

## Trial and promotion

Use a distinct reviewer for trial/promotion. Give them source artifacts and the applicable
goal, not merely the writer's desired conclusion. They should consider:

- Is the statement a mechanism, or just a local choice disguised as a rule?
- What conditions make it useful; when should it not be applied?
- Does the use/outcome pair support benefit versus a credible baseline?
- Was the relevant failure/counterexample actually checked?
- Does it conflict with a newer instruction or source?

The CLI enforces exact revisions and a minimum evidence gate; it cannot judge the semantic
truth of an arbitrary text field. A one-case promotion is scoped to the tested conditions,
not proof of generality. Repeated independent results may broaden confidence, never silently
broaden authority. No numeric score is a substitute for source review.

Reviewers record checks and a counterexample even when the outcome is negative. Harmful
observations suspend guidance so it leaves default recall immediately. Rework the claim as a
new candidate revision, or retire it. Never erase the negative result to make the next trial
look better. Old revisions stay available for audit but not normal retrieval.

## Portable learning without project contamination

Cross-project transfer is explicit. Retain the source project/revision in provenance; remove
private names, customer facts and architecture assumptions from the candidate wording.
Substitute other product nouns: if the statement stops making sense, it is probably local
context rather than a reusable mechanism. Inspiration can travel freely; factual constraints
must be established again in the destination. Do not copy a user decision as a general rule.

Existing project EXPERIENCE.md ledgers can supply episode evidence. TaskDock, repository
decision records, and work-memory have different roles: continuity, canonical truth, and
selective retrieval/learning. Do not mirror every ledger into every system.

## Review when there is evidence, not on a ceremonial schedule

Review at a material correction, an outcome arriving, a repeated failure, source drift,
or a user-requested audit/export. Check pending trials while re-entering relevant work.
If no meaningful delta exists, retain a no-change checkpoint and continue the actual task.
Do not interrupt the user with a status message for every memory operation.
